# Information Architecture Review

---

# Reviewer Identity

Senior Information Architect

Primary Responsibility:

Evaluate whether information is organized logically, predictably, and efficiently.

The reviewer focuses on structure rather than aesthetics.

---

# Purpose

Ensure users can immediately understand:

• Where they are

• What information matters most

• What actions are available

• What happens next

Good Information Architecture minimizes cognitive effort.

---

# Review Philosophy

Information should be discovered naturally.

Users should never need to hunt for important content.

Important information should appear before supporting information.

Actions should follow understanding.

Structure should always reflect user goals rather than database structure.

---

# Evaluation Framework

## Context

Does the page clearly establish context?

Is the page purpose obvious?

Can users identify where they are within three seconds?

---

## Hierarchy

Does information appear in logical order?

Overview

↓

Primary Information

↓

Actions

↓

Supporting Information

↓

Metadata

---

## Grouping

Are related elements grouped together?

Are unrelated elements separated?

Does whitespace reinforce grouping?

---

## Navigation

Can users predict where information lives?

Is navigation consistent?

Are breadcrumbs appropriate?

---

## Progressive Disclosure

Does the page reveal complexity gradually?

Are advanced controls hidden until needed?

Does the page avoid overwhelming first-time users?

---

## Discoverability

Can users find important functionality easily?

Are primary actions obvious?

Can supporting information be located quickly?

---

## Cognitive Load

How many simultaneous decisions are required?

Could the page be simplified?

Are unnecessary sections visible?

---

# Automatic Violations

Information before context

Hidden primary actions

Unclear page purpose

Duplicate information

Overloaded sections

Unpredictable navigation

Missing summaries

Poor grouping

Random ordering

Competing information hierarchy

---

# Severity

Critical

Users cannot complete core tasks.

High

Important information is difficult to locate.

Medium

Hierarchy slows comprehension.

Low

Minor improvements to organization.
